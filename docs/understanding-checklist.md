# Acute Encephalopathy App: Understanding Checklist

Status key: [ ] not yet · [~] partly · [x] shown understanding

## Stage 1: The problem
- [ ] Why two groups (delirium vs encephalopathy) ended up using different words for the same thing
- [ ] Why that split matters for patients (labels steer care, early closure, "AMS" says nothing)
- [ ] Process vs presentation: encephalopathy = what's wrong inside; subsyndromal delirium / delirium / coma = what you see
- [ ] The four branches: not new (baseline), coma, delirium, subsyndromal delirium
- [ ] What the 2020 paper did NOT provide (no bedside criteria, no cut-offs), and why that is the gap this app fills

## Stage 2: The solution
- [ ] The five steps and why they come in this order (arousal before features)
- [ ] Shortcut 1: "not new" goes straight to the result
- [ ] Shortcut 2: coma (RASS −4/−5) skips the feature questions
- [ ] The rules table, and why checking order matters
- [ ] Why low/moderate/high and not a percentage
- [ ] Edge cases: unsure baseline (one level lower), RASS −3, dementia switch, several causes, "not yet known" cause, red flag shown even when likelihood is low, sedation
- [ ] How the chart sentence is built and why it names both cause and presentation
- [ ] Design choices: one question per screen, color meaning, 44 pt targets, contrast, reduced motion

## Stage 3: Broader context
- [ ] What the app will change (shared vocabulary, fewer "AMS" notes, prompt to find a cause, coding)
- [ ] What it does not do (doesn't replace 4AT/CAM-ICU, not validated, decision support only)
- [ ] Why test patients (Phase 3) must come before a pilot
- [ ] Which single setting a clinician reviewer is most likely to challenge (the coma cut-off) and where it lives
